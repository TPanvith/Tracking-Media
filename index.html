Method & limitations

How to read the numbers

Open methodology ↗

Allocation uses the Union Budget demand for grants. Releases and reported expenditure are transcribed from published government records. Beneficiaries are cumulative programme-reported counts; uniqueness and overlap are not independently established.

Per-beneficiary values are arithmetic ratios only. ₹ crore × 10,000,000 ÷ reported count. They do not establish individual receipt, service quality or causal impact.

Known gaps: district-level disaggregation, unique beneficiary count, household denominator and matched-period outcomes.

SOURCE RECORDS · SAMPLE DATA

Data should travel with its evidence

Each source record preserves what was used, when it was captured and which values it supports. This prototype uses illustrative scheme figures to demonstrate the interface; they are not live-verified official data.

Sample tracker methodology

Coverage: central scheme budget allocation, releases, reported expenditure and beneficiary count. Currency displayed in Indian rupees; totals in crore.

Calculation: execution ratio = reported expenditure ÷ allocation. Release ratio = reported releases ÷ allocation. Per-reported-beneficiary spending = reported expenditure converted to rupees ÷ reported count. These calculations are descriptive ratios only.

Period handling: Each metric displays the source period. Figures from different reporting cutoffs are never silently combined; period mismatches are shown beside derived measures.

Uncertainty: Missing denominators, unverified uniqueness, absent outcome series and conflicting records must be labeled. No value is imputed into a blank field. Corrections preserve the old version and its reason for change.
